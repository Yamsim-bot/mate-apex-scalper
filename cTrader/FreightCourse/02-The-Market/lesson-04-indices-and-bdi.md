# Lesson 04 — The indices: BDI, BCI, BPI and friends

**Goal:** understand how a messy market becomes a number, and be able to read the Baltic's daily report.

## Who publishes the numbers

The **Baltic Exchange** (London; owned by the Singapore Exchange since 2016) publishes daily assessments of representative routes. Panellists (freight brokers) report what each route *would* cost, and the Baltic averages their submissions into an index number published each London afternoon.

## The four dry-bulk sub-indices

Each vessel class has an index made from an average of key routes:

| Index | Vessel class | Made from |
|---|---|---|
| **BCI** | Capesize (~180k t) | 5 time-charter routes — **BCI 5TC** ($/day) |
| **BPI** | Panamax (~82k t) | 5 time-charter routes — **BPI 5TC** ($/day) |
| **BSI** | Supramax (~58k t) | 10 time-charter routes — **BSI 10TC** ($/day) |
| **BHSI** | Handysize (~38k t) | 7 time-charter routes — **BHSI 7TC** ($/day) |

A "TC route" is a standard voyage, expressed as an earning in **$/day** (a time-charter equivalent).

## The BDI

**BDI = Baltic Dry Index** — the headline composite everyone quotes in the news. Since the March 2018 re-weighting it is a weighted average of the sub-indices, roughly **40% Capesize / 30% Panamax / 30% Supramax**. (The exact formula has changed over the years — what matters is that it's the "dry bulk market in one number".)

The BDI is an **index number** (points), not a price — it shows direction and magnitude, not dollars.

Famous voyage-rate routes you'll see quoted in **$/tonne**:

- **C5** — W. Australia → Qingdao, iron ore (~170k t cargo)
- **C3** — Brazil (Tubarão) → Qingdao, iron ore
- **P3A** — Panamax transatlantic round voyage

## How to read a daily Baltic report (30 seconds)

1. **BDI** — the headline. Up/down tells you the market mood.
2. **BCI / BPI / BSI / BHSI** — which class is moving? Often one class drives the day.
3. **TC rates ($/day)** — the actual money: is a Capesize earning $12k or $45k a day?
4. **Voyage routes ($/t)** — the cost side for cargo owners (C5, C3…).
5. **% change** — magnitude, not just direction.

## Why indices matter for trading

Indices are the **settlement basis** for Forward Freight Agreements (Lesson 06). You never have to find a ship — you trade the index itself, cash-settled.

## Key takeaways

- The Baltic Exchange publishes daily route assessments; averaged panellist submissions become indices.
- BCI/BPI/BSI/BHSI cover the four dry-bulk classes; **BDI is their weighted composite** (~40/30/30 since 2018).
- Read a report in order: BDI → sub-indices → TC rates → voyage rates.
- Indices are the raw material for FFA trading — they're what contracts settle against.
