# Lesson 02 — Who trades freight, and why?

**Goal:** meet the four types of players and understand the core motive: **hedging** vs **speculation**.

## The four players

| Player | What they have | Why they trade |
|---|---|---|
| **Shipowner** | The physical ship | Wants guaranteed income; worried rates will fall before the ship is chartered |
| **Charterer / cargo owner** (miners, mills, grain traders) | The cargo | Wants predictable shipping cost; worried rates will rise |
| **Trading house / fund** | Capital | No ship, no cargo — just a view on where rates are going (speculation) |
| **Broker** | Relationships + market data | Matches ships to cargoes (physical) and buyers to sellers (paper); earns commission |

## The two motives — always ask "which one is this?"

- **Hedging** = *reducing* an existing risk. The shipowner whose ship will be empty in December is exposed to December rates falling. Hedging locks in today's rate for December.
- **Speculation** = *taking* price risk for profit. The fund with no ship buys December freight because it thinks rates will rise.

> Rule of thumb: **hedgers have ships or cargo; speculators have opinions.** The exact same trade (buying or selling a December freight contract) can be a hedge for one person and a bet for another.

## Why the two sides need each other

If every owner expected rates to rise, there'd be no one to take the other side. The market works because owners and charterers have *opposite* natural risks — and that's what makes it liquid enough for speculators to join.

## Brokers and the Baltic

Freight has no central trading floor. Rates are agreed between brokers (representing owners and charterers) and reported to the Baltic Exchange's **panellists**, who turn the day's deals into published index numbers. That's how a decentralized market gets a single daily price — we'll see the indices in Lesson 04.

## Key takeaways

- Four players: owners, charterers, speculators, brokers.
- Hedging reduces risk you already have; speculation takes risk for profit.
- Owners and charterers are natural opposites — that's the liquidity.
- The Baltic Exchange aggregates broker deals into daily index numbers.
