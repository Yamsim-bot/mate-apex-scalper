# Lesson 01 — What is freight?

**Goal:** define freight, see the two big markets, and get the "cargo space" idea.

## The one-sentence definition

**Freight is the price of moving goods by sea.** When a steel mill in China needs iron ore from Australia, someone has to pay a ship to carry it. That price — per tonne, or per day of the ship's time — is the freight rate.

## Ships sell cargo space, not cargo

A shipowner doesn't sell the iron ore. They sell *space and time* on a ship. Think of a ship as a floating warehouse that rents itself out:

- **Voyage rate** — a price per tonne for one specific trip (e.g. "iron ore, W. Australia → Qingdao: $10.50/t").
- **Time-charter (TC) rate** — a price per day for the whole ship (e.g. "a Panamax: $14,000/day for 6 months").

Rates are quoted daily by the **Baltic Exchange** in London — the freight market's equivalent of a stock exchange.

## The two big markets

| Market | What it moves | Example cargoes |
|---|---|---|
| **Dry bulk** | Raw materials in bulk | Iron ore, coal, grains, fertilizers |
| **Tankers** | Liquid cargo | Crude oil, refined products |

There's also container shipping (boxed goods), but the classic *tradable* freight indices (the BDI) are dry bulk — so this course focuses there.

## Why it matters

1. **It's global trade's barometer.** If China stops buying iron ore, ships sit idle and rates collapse. The BDI is famously a leading indicator of world economic activity.
2. **It's real money.** A single Capesize (the largest dry-bulk ship) can earn or lose tens of thousands of dollars *per day* as rates swing.
3. **It's learnable.** Unlike a stock, the "company" is the whole world fleet — supply and demand of ships is something you can actually reason about.

## Key takeaways

- Freight = the price of shipping goods by sea (per tonne, or per day).
- Dry bulk (iron ore, coal, grain) is the classic market; tankers move oil.
- The Baltic Exchange publishes the daily rates everyone trades on.
- Freight is driven by ship supply vs. cargo demand — we build on that in every later lesson.
