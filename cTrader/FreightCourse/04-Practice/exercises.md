## Module 1 — Foundations

**Q1.** Match each cargo to its most likely vessel class: (a) iron ore Australia → China, (b) wheat from the Black Sea, (c) crude Middle East → Asia, (d) fertilizer to a small port.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(a) Capesize, (b) Panamax/Supramax, (c) VLCC, (d) Handysize/Supramax.</details>

**Q2.** In one sentence: what's the difference between a voyage rate and a time-charter rate?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Voyage = $/tonne for one specific trip; TC = $/day for the ship's time, regardless of cargo.</details>

**Q3.** A shipowner and a hedge fund both *sell* a December FFA. Is it a hedge for both? Why / why not?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Only for the owner, who has the ship's future earnings at risk. The fund has no underlying exposure — that's speculation.</details>

## Module 2 — The Market

**Q4.** What does the BDI measure, and roughly how is it composed?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The headline dry-bulk freight index — a weighted composite of the sub-indices, roughly 40% BCI (Capesize) + 30% BPI (Panamax) + 30% BSI (Supramax) since the 2018 re-weighting.</details>

**Q5.** Why does a Brazil → China iron-ore cargo consume more market "capacity" than an Australia → China one?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>The voyage is ~3× longer, so one ship is tied up far longer — fewer round trips per year, so more fleet capacity consumed per tonne (tonne-miles).</details>

**Q6.** Name three supply-side factors that can tighten the market (raise rates).

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Any three: port congestion, slow steaming, scrapping, weather delays (cyclones/rains), canal disruptions, low orderbook.</details>

**Q7.** Why is Q4 typically the strongest quarter for dry bulk?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Iron-ore restocking into winter, North-American grain exports, and weather risks — demand rises into year-end while supply is stretched.</details>

## Module 3 — FFA

**Q8.** You *buy* 1 lot of Dec BPI 5TC at $15,000/day. December's average settles at $18,000/day. December has 31 days. What's your P&L?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(18,000 − 15,000) × 31 = **+$93,000** (long, rates rose → profit).</details>

**Q9.** Same contract, but you were **short** and December averages $13,000/day. P&L?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(15,000 − 13,000) × 31 = **+$62,000** (short, rates fell → profit).</details>

**Q10.** A charterer needs 170,000 t of iron ore moved on route C5 in June. She buys the June C5 FFA at $10.50/t. June C5 averages $12.25/t. What does she receive, and what did she effectively lock in?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>(12.25 − 10.50) × 170,000 = **+$297,500** received. She effectively locked in ~$10.50/t freight cost — the FFA gain offsets the higher spot rate she pays for the physical cargo.</details>

**Q11.** The December FFA trades *above* the March FFA. Is the curve in contango or backwardation, and what does the market expect?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>Dec > Mar means the curve declines into the future → **backwardation**. The market prices Q4 strength and Q1 weakness — exactly the seasonal pattern from Lesson 05.</details>
